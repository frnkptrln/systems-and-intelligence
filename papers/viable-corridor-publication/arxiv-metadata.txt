Abstract (LaTeX inline notation):
The Thermodynamics of Emergent Orchestration (TEO) combines replicator resource competition, Kuramoto value coupling, a homeostatic brake and substrate dissipation. Under stated assumptions, positive regulation ($\gamma>0$), above-onset coupling ($K>K_c$) and bounded accumulated overshoot ($\Omega(t)<S_{\max}$) are componentwise necessary for robust long-time viability. This structural result reflects the defined conjunction of constraints. The substantive claim is that capability can load several constraints simultaneously. A deterministic ODE and stochastic agent ecology demonstrate exits through multiple boundaries and recovery through joint strengthening where single-axis responses fail. Their shared author and toolchain limit the independence of this evidence. Sufficiency is not established: the conjunction is refuted in its Lorentzian continuum reading because above-onset coupling need not meet the coherence floor (erratum, §3.4). A population-specific, floor-dependent correction remains open. An uncalibrated structural-isomorphism hypothesis relates AI optimization and civilizational regimes. Predictions distinguish model checks, empirical mappings and future real-agent tests. No real system has been shown to meet the proposed definition.
