# Mechanism as a Reconstruction Target

**Status:** Exploratory note — not a repository claim.

**Trigger.** Posner, Lei, and Schölkopf argue for mechanistic world models that aim beyond prediction toward reusable mechanisms.

**Question.** What would let this repository call a recovered process a *mechanism* rather than merely a predictive model?

A useful criterion may be **transport under intervention**. A candidate mechanism should not only fit observed traces; its typed parts and relations should support counterfactual reuse when components, couplings, or contexts change. This would make mechanism a stricter reconstruction target than predictive equivalence, while avoiding the vague claim that any compact latent structure is explanatory.

**Probe.** Compare predictive and explicitly modular process models on a small family of coupled finite systems. Fit both on the same observations, then test held-out interventions and recombinations. Ask whether the proposed decomposition predicts changes that a matched black-box predictor does not.

**Failure condition.** If the mechanistic representation adds no transportable predictive or intervention advantage, the extra vocabulary may be descriptive rather than explanatory.

**Source:** Posner, Lei & Schölkopf (2026), *From Observation to Insight: Mechanistic World Models and the Quest for Autonomous Discovery*, arXiv:2607.12474.
