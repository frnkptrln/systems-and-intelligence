# Collective Self-Knowledge May Require Synergy

**Status:** exploratory note — not a repository claim

Self-reference is cheap. A system can store a description of itself, repeat its goals, or predict its own outputs without any information being genuinely distributed across its parts.

Krti Tallam's uncommon self-knowledge proposal suggests a sharper diagnostic: ask whether information about the system's own next state exists only in the joint state of multiple components and disappears when those components are considered separately. Partial Information Decomposition provides one candidate language for this distinction between redundant, unique, and synergistic information.

The repository should not inherit the stronger claim that positive self-directed synergy is a criterion for consciousness. A safer use is architectural: **does a collective possess predictive self-information that cannot be recovered from its components independently?**

That quantity could be compared with commit-time composition, global availability, NTIC-style closure, and intervention response. If synergy appears equally in trivial coupled systems, it is not sufficient for agency either; the value would then be diagnostic rather than constitutive.
